# Letter Structure

The anatomy below follows the Illinois Tech Career Services Cover Letter Guide (2020).
The framework is theirs; the wording and the example letter here are original.

## The page, top to bottom

1. **Heading block** — the candidate's name and contact line, matching the résumé header
   exactly. A letter set in a different typeface than the résumé it arrives with looks
   assembled from parts.
2. **Date** — the date the letter is sent, written out: `March 3, 2026`.
3. **Company name** — on its own line.
4. **Salutation** — `Dear First Last,` when the hiring manager is known, `Dear Hiring
   Manager,` when they are not. Never `To Whom It May Concern,` which announces that
   nobody looked.
5. **Introduction** — the hook.
6. **Body** — one or two paragraphs.
7. **Conclusion** — one paragraph, four jobs.
8. **Closing** — `Kind regards,` / `Sincerely,` / `Respectfully,` then the typed name.

## Introduction: the hook

Open with something that makes the reader keep reading. Four methods, each with what it
requires from the candidate's material:

| Method | What it needs |
|---|---|
| A relevant story or anecdote | A Narrative entry describing a concrete event |
| A bold or interesting statement about the candidate | A Narrative entry, or a strong Project/Experience fact |
| A question that makes the reader think | A Narrative motivation plus a theme from the posting |
| Mentioning an employee who recommended them | A Narrative referral entry, including the person's name |

Naming the position is allowed. It is not the point of the paragraph, and it must not be
the first clause.

**Never open with:** `I am writing to apply for…`, `I am writing in response to…`,
`Please accept my application for…`, `My name is…`, or any first clause whose subject is
the posting rather than the candidate.

If no material supports any of the four methods, ask the candidate before drafting. A
fabricated anecdote is the worst failure this skill can produce.

## Body

**Do not restate the résumé.** The reader has it. The body's job is the *why* and the
*how* that a bullet has no room for: what the problem actually was, what the candidate
decided, what they learned, why it bears on this posting.

Each body paragraph should do exactly one of these:

- Tie one concrete piece of the candidate's experience to one key point in the posting.
- Explain a motivation or a turn in their path that the résumé leaves unexplained.
- Show understanding of the role and the organisation, built on a usable specific per
  [company-research.md](../../../shared/company-research.md).

## Conclusion

One paragraph doing four things, in this order:

1. A final statement about employability for this role.
2. Thanks for reading.
3. An explicit desire for an interview.
4. The best way to make contact.

Dropping any of the four leaves the letter unfinished. This paragraph is never what gets
cut to reach one page.

## Annotated example

Fictional throughout. It shows the shape and the register; it is not a form to fill in —
a letter that reads like a filled-in form is the failure this file exists to prevent.

> **Priya Example**
> Chicago, IL • priya@example.edu • (312) 555-0142
>
> March 3, 2026
>
> Example Water Authority
>
> Dear Marcus Example,
>
> *[Hook — anecdote, traced to a Narrative entry]*
> The first data pipeline I ever built was a spreadsheet my mother used to track water
> bills for the six-flat she managed. It was terrible. It also caught a billing error
> that had been running for two years, and I have been chasing that particular feeling
> ever since.
>
> *[Body — one experience against one posting requirement, supplying the why]*
> Your posting asks for someone who can take sensor data from the field and make it
> trustworthy before anyone reports on it. That was most of my work at the campus
> facilities lab. The meters were fine; they just dropped readings whenever the network
> flapped, and every downstream report quietly averaged over the gaps. I rebuilt the
> ingestion so the gaps stayed gaps. The dashboards got uglier and the numbers got
> honest, which took some explaining.
>
> *[Body — organisation understanding, from a usable specific]*
> I read the source-water assessment you publish. The section on aging meter
> infrastructure is the part of this job I want — not the modelling, but the unglamorous
> work of making a measurement mean what it says it means.
>
> *[Conclusion — employability, thanks, interview, contact]*
> You would be getting an engineer who has already argued for accuracy over a prettier
> chart, and who wants to do it somewhere the measurement matters. Thank you for reading.
> I would welcome the chance to talk about the role, and can be reached at
> priya@example.edu or (312) 555-0142.
>
> Kind regards,
>
> Priya Example

Note what the example does not do: it never restates a résumé bullet, it never claims a
history with the employer beyond reading one public document, and its hook comes from a
fact about the candidate's life that no amount of company research could have supplied.
